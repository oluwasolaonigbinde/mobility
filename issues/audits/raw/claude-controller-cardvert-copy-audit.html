Product content audit · master @ 3832cff · read-only

Cardvert is talking to its own build system.

Across the public site, the driver app, the advertiser workspace and every outbound email, the product speaks in the vocabulary of the delivery process that built it — authority, governance, freezing, manifests, digests, gates. Drivers are shown SHA-256 hashes. Advertisers are told to “issue a new governed measurement run.” Applicants are asked to save a UUID. This is a copy audit of what is on screen, with replacement wording and a voice guide to stop it recurring.

Defects, not taste

How severity is assigned. Blocker means an external user — a driver, an applicant, a paying advertiser — is shown something they cannot act on, cannot understand, or that damages trust in the money or the measurement. High is comprehensible but wrong in voice. Medium is inconsistency and polish.

Findings tagged Defect are mechanical: a raw enum reaching the screen, a slug rendered as a label, a message referencing a code that is never displayed. Those are bugs and should be fixed regardless of any view on tone. Everything else is a content judgement and is open to argument.

Part A

Part C

Cardvert — the driver app

This is the worst-affected surface and the highest-stakes one: it is used one-handed, in traffic, by people whose weekly income depends on understanding it. It currently speaks in the vocabulary of a distributed-systems design document.

Cardvert voice guide

Short enough to be held in mind while writing a string. The test for any line: would you say it out loud to a driver at a filling station in Abuja, or to a brand manager across a table?

Brand voice

Direct, warm, and specific. We are the counterparty, not the platform. We say we and you — never “the system”, “operations”, “ops”, or “Cardvert” as the subject of a sentence about our own behaviour.

Rigour is shown, not named. Our measurement and payout discipline is a differentiator. Express it as a promise the reader benefits from — “these figures are locked and won't change” — never as the mechanism that delivers it.

Confident about the pilot. Ten cars in Abuja for three months is a specific, credible thing. Never imply scale we don't have.

Sentence style

Active voice. British English. One idea per sentence, under 20 words where possible. Sentence case for every heading, label and button.

Lead with the outcome, then the reason. “We can't start your trip — your car's documents need renewing”, not the reverse.

Not thisCampaign work remains unavailable until every server-governed stage completes.
ThisA few things still need finishing before you can start earning. They're listed below.

Terminology

One word per concept, held across app, email and marketing site:

job (not assignment) · offer · trip · base zone / premium zone / excluded (never tier) · review (never fraud, in any driver-facing string) · payout · documents (never evidence, to drivers) · campaign · advert (the thing on the car) · creative (the artwork, advertiser-side only)

Money is always ₦ with thousands separators. Durations are always 2h 14m — never raw seconds. Dates are 12 Sep 2026. Times carry WAT, never a timezone identifier.

Buttons and controls

A button is a verb phrase naming the outcome in the user's language: Start trip, Accept job, Download CSV, Take photo. Never a system verb (Reconcile, Deactivate, Retry the same request) and never a bare noun.

Consequences go in helper text, not the label. The button says Accept these terms; the line beneath says Once accepted, these terms can't be changed.

Loading states use the button's own verb in the present participle — Starting…, Uploading… — never an internal phase name.

Destructive and irreversible actions name what stops, and when.

Empty states

Three parts: what would be here, why it isn't yet, and the one thing that would change that. Never a bare negation.

Not thisNo canonical receipts have been recorded.
ThisNo payments yet. Once you transfer, we'll list it here within one working day.

Errors

Own the fault when it is ours. Say what happened in the user's terms, whether their work or money is safe, and exactly one thing to do next. Never expose the internal cause, a stack of negations, or an instruction only staff can carry out.

If you tell someone to contact support, show the reference and name the channel in the same sentence.

Not thisFrozen analysis failed its integrity check. Issue a new governed measurement run after correcting the source authority.
ThisWe're holding this report back — our checks flagged something in the underlying data. Our team has been alerted and will be in touch.

Blocked and gated states

The hardest category in this product, and the one currently written worst. Every blocked state must answer three questions in order:

1. Is this me or you?   2. What happens to my money or my work in the meantime?   3. What is the single next thing — and by when?

If the block is caused by our own inconsistent data, say so plainly and give a human to contact. Never show the driver the contradiction.

Not thisActivation authority conflict — Assignment history looks active but canonical activation is absent.
ThisSomething doesn't add up on your account, so we can't start a trip yet. We've flagged it — call us on [number] if you need it today.

Emails

Subject line names the thing and the event: “Yello Season Q3 has paused — budget reached”. Never a bare category.

Body: greeting with first name, what happened, what it means for them, one link. Footer: who sent it, why they got it, how to change preferences.

Never put an internal risk category (“fraud”) in a subject line. Never send a message about someone's money that omits the amount and the date.

Status labels

Every enum that reaches the screen passes through an exhaustive Record<Status, string> so the compiler forces a label for each new state. No underscore-stripping fallbacks — they let untranslated values ship silently.

Labels name the user's situation, not the record's: Waiting on us, Action needed, Under review, Paid.

Cardvert vs Terrax Media

Terrax Media is the company. It sells campaigns to brands, contracts with advertisers, issues quotations and invoices, and publishes the Campaign Performance Analysis. It owns the public site, the advertiser workspace, the admin tools, and every advertiser-facing email. Its line is Driving Impact Beyond Locations.

Cardvert is the driver app, and only the driver app. It appears on /driver/*, on the driver application funnel, and in driver emails. Drivers work with Cardvert; brands work with Terrax Media.

Where they meet: the landing page's Cardvert section, and the driver's offer, which comes from a Terrax Media campaign. In both places, name the relationship once — “Cardvert is Terrax Media's driver app” — and then use one name per surface.

Never: a shared sign-in branded Cardvert for advertisers; “Terrax measurement” as a product name; “the network”, “the platform”, “command center” or “workspace” as substitutes for either.
